Massie files 8 impeachment articles against Hegseth; House vote by Wednesday

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) reintroduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Nov. 9 as a privileged measure, forcing a House floor vote within two legislative days (by Nov. 11) unless Republicans kill it by majority motion. Three charges cite Iran War Powers Resolution issues; others cite alleged drug-boat strikes, Maduro capture, and Yemen. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Sunday he is a hard yes. Hegseth scheduled a Pentagon press conference for Tuesday morning. Same day, Trump told reporters the Iran war is militarily effectively over (AP).

Quick facts

  • Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) reintroduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on Monday, Nov. 9. (NBC; NPR; Washington Post)
  • He filed the measure as privileged under House rules, which forces a floor vote within two legislative days — by Wednesday, Nov. 11 — unless Republicans kill it with a majority motion. (NBC; PolitiFact)
  • Three of the eight charges accuse Hegseth of waging the Iran war without a declaration of war or statutory authorization (War Powers Resolution). Other charges cite alleged drug-boat strikes, the capture of Venezuela’s Nicolás Maduro, and military action in Yemen. (NBC; NPR)
  • House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said Sunday he is a “hard yes” on the impeachment effort. (NBC)
  • Hegseth said Monday he will hold a Pentagon press conference Tuesday morning on the impeachment effort. (NBC)

Not yet confirmed

  • Whether the privileged resolution reaches a floor vote or is tabled / killed by a Republican majority motion before Wednesday. Outlets report both paths are available; no final procedural outcome yet. (NBC; PolitiFact)
  • How many Republicans would join Democrats if a floor vote is held. PolitiFact notes analysts’ vote-math scenarios; Rep. Don Bacon (R-Neb.) has said he is undecided. These are projections, not a counted whip. (PolitiFact)
  • Whether a House passage would lead to Senate conviction/removal. No Senate trial schedule exists; conviction would require a two-thirds Senate vote under the Constitution (standard impeachment process — not yet triggered).

Related same-day development

  • After meeting Qatar’s prime minister, President Trump told reporters Monday the war in Iran is “militarily effectively over” and that the U.S. is keeping a “status quo,” while saying forces remain ready if circumstances change. (AP; Washington Post)

What happens next

  • Tuesday morning: Hegseth Pentagon press conference on the impeachment effort.
  • By Wednesday, Nov. 11: House must consider the privileged resolution unless it is killed by a majority motion.
  • If the House approves articles, the matter goes to the Senate for trial.
