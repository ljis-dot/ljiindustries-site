Anthropic's IPO could value it at up to $1.4 trillion

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude AI models, plans to price its initial public offering on Wednesday, Nov. 18, and begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on Thursday, Nov. 19, under the ticker "ANT." Its amended filing sets a range of $70 to $78 a share for 825 million Class A shares (about 801 million from the company and about 24 million from existing holders), which would raise $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion. At the top of the range, the company would be worth about $1.4 trillion on a fully diluted basis (CNBC: up to $1.405 trillion; TechCrunch: $1.404 trillion), up from the $965 billion valuation in its May funding round but below the roughly $2 trillion it was reportedly targeting earlier. CNBC reported that Anthropic is aiming to allocate about 20% of the offering to retail investors. Yahoo Finance reported a $26 billion private placement alongside the IPO, including up to $10 billion each from Nvidia and Alphabet. The IPO lands in the same week as Nvidia's quarterly results, due after the close on Tuesday, Nov. 17. Yahoo Finance and Reuters (via Investing.com) framed the two events as the week's big tests for the AI trade.

Quick facts

  • Ticker ANT, Nasdaq; IPO price set Wednesday, Nov. 18; trading expected Thursday, Nov. 19
  • 825 million shares at $70–$78; up to $64.35 billion raised
  • About $1.4 trillion fully diluted at $78 (the value on shares outstanding after the IPO is lower, about $1 trillion)
  • Nvidia earnings: Tuesday, Nov. 17, after the close

Not yet confirmed

  • The final IPO price and first-day trading
  • Whether the underwriters exercise their option on 123.75 million more shares

U.S. · Starbase, TXReported only

SpaceX catches a Starship returning from orbit for the first time

SpaceX caught the Starship upper stage, Ship 42, with the "chopstick" arms of its Starbase, Texas, launch tower on Saturday at about 4:20 p.m. ET, ending Flight 15. The ship deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites after reaching orbit (SpaceX, CNBC, Space.com, Xinhua) and circled Earth 14 times (BBC, CNBC, Aerospace America) before returning about 22.5 hours after Friday's liftoff (SpaceX, BBC, Space.com; AP said 22 hours). SpaceX's flight log puts the catch at T+22:32:34; reports of "about 24 hours" are not supported by these sources. It was the first time SpaceX has caught the upper stage; it had caught the Super Heavy booster on three earlier flights. The booster did not make it back. SpaceX said its boostback burn ended early on low fuel, the booster aborted its catch and diverted itself offshore, hovered over the Gulf after a landing burn, and its flight termination system was then triggered. CNBC, AP and BBC described it as exploding over the water.

Quick facts

  • Ship 42 caught at Starbase on Saturday, Nov. 14, about 22.5 hours after launch
  • 26 Starlink V3 satellites deployed; 14 orbits
  • First Starship upper-stage catch; booster catch aborted, booster lost over the Gulf

Not yet confirmed

  • The root cause of the booster's low-fuel abort (SpaceX says it is under investigation)

U.S. · New OrleansReported only

All 197 survive after a tanker hits a docked river cruise ship in New Orleans

The tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck American Cruise Lines' American Heritage at about 12:30 p.m. Friday as the riverboat was docked and disembarking passengers on the Mississippi River in New Orleans. The cruise ship rolled onto its side, and about 100 people were pulled from the water. All 197 passengers and crew survived, AP reported. Officials said the rescue was led by Harbor Police, the New Orleans Fire Department, New Orleans police, the U.S. Coast Guard and medical personnel; Coast Guard crews ferried passengers to a dock (NOLA.com), the Coast Guard was among the agencies on scene conducting rescue operations, and American Cruise Lines thanked the Coast Guard (Fox News). Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the Coast Guard was supporting state and local responders. Officials said 32 people were taken to hospitals, including two police officers, and eight remained hospitalized Saturday; NBC reported two of the eight were in serious condition, while AP said none of the injuries were critical. Coast Guard Capt. Melanie Burnham said initial reports pointed to a steering problem on the tanker. The NTSB said it has recovered the tanker's voyage data recorder; a 16-member team expects to spend a week on site, with a preliminary report in about 30 days. The Port of New Orleans sued, and a judge issued a warrant giving federal officials control of the tanker.

Quick facts

  • 197 aboard the American Heritage; all survived
  • Rescue led by Harbor Police, firefighters, NOPD and the Coast Guard (officials, via NOLA.com and Fox News)
  • 32 hospitalized; 8 still hospitalized Saturday
  • NTSB has the tanker's voyage data recorder; preliminary report expected in roughly 30 days

Not yet confirmed

  • The cause of the collision (initial reports point to a steering problem)

U.S. · Washington, DCReported only

Judge keeps East Potomac Park and its golf course open

U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes on Saturday issued a temporary restraining order barring the Trump administration from closing East Potomac Park and East Potomac Golf Course during their usual hours, ahead of a closure planned for Sunday tied to the president's planned redevelopment of the public course. The order runs through Nov. 28, or until further order. It does not require the government to operate the course for play or hire an operator, and it allows routine closures and repairs. The Washington Post reported that Reyes found the government's stated reasons for the closure were "pretextual."

U.S. · Washington, DCReported only

Trump backs a bipartisan federal film tax credit

The bill is bipartisan: S. 5552 was introduced by Sen. Tim Scott (R-S.C.) for himself and Sens. Adam Schiff (D-Calif.), John Cornyn (R-Texas) and Raphael Warnock (D-Ga.), per the bill text, and The Hill lists Democratic and Republican senators Trump named as partners. A companion House effort was announced by Reps. Nathaniel Moran (R), Linda Sanchez (D), Brian Jack (R) and Laura Friedman (D) (Schiff release). President Trump said Friday on Truth Social that he is working with Sen. Tim Scott and, "believe it or not, Senator Adam Schiff," plus a bipartisan group of senators and House members, on the Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act (S. 5552). The bill, introduced Sept. 24 and referred to the Senate Finance Committee, would create a 20% federal tax credit on qualified U.S. labor costs for film and TV productions that spend more than $1 million and shoot at least 75% of principal photography days in the U.S. Bonus credits of 5 points each, for rural opportunity zones, disaster areas, independent producers, multi-state producers and increased domestic production, can raise it to 30%.

U.S. · Strait of HormuzReported only

Iran says three tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's navy and Revolutionary Guards navy said three oil tankers caught fire Saturday after striking naval mines in and around the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian state media reported. The Guards said two tankers had switched off their radar and tracking systems and were using an unauthorized route; one was described as a supertanker. The regular navy said a third tanker hit a mine in a prohibited zone east of the strait. The ships were not named. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the reports. The U.S. military has said it cleared the main routes through the waterway of mines (CNA).

Iranian state media.
