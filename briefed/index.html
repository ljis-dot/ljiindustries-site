Push grows to bring Jensen Huang back to Nvidia

A "Stand with Jensen" online petition calling for Jensen Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO has passed 170,000 signatures, CNBC reported Monday (City A.M. put it at more than 160,000 over the weekend), with Elon Musk, Y Combinator's Garry Tan and Epic Games' Tim Sweeney among its backers. Musk posted on X that he was signing it. The Financial Times reported that Huang has discussed a possible return with close confidants, according to CNBC and City A.M. Huang resigned last week; longtime CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board looks at internal and external candidates. An Nvidia spokesperson said the company is selecting a search firm and has not formally engaged any candidates. Nvidia told CNBC that speculation about individual candidates, internal or external, is premature. Nvidia shares were lower in premarket trading Monday (CNBC; The Guardian described a resumed AI sell-off). Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results on Tuesday, Nov. 17, with Kress as interim CEO. CNBC's Monday morning newsletter put the petition at more than 175,000.

  • The petition's count is self-reported by its organizers and is not independently verified
  • Whether the board would consider a return

U.S. · El Dorado, Ark.Reported only

Duffy: Friday's ground stop began with a router at an Arkansas FAA radio site

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said on NBC's "Meet the Press" and ABC's "This Week" on Sunday that Friday's nationwide ground stop began during work at a remote FAA radio facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, where an old copper line was being replaced with fiber. When the site was turned back on, he said, its router flooded the rest of the FAA network with data; a site in Amarillo, Texas, upgraded about three weeks earlier, also began flooding the system. Duffy said investigators are trying to determine whether it was "a faulty installation by L3Harris," the contractor that did the work and owns the FAA's communications network, or a fault in the router itself. He said there was no indication of a cyberattack. The router is being examined at an L3Harris lab in Florida, and the FAA has switched off the broadcast function on 69 similar routers.

U.S. · Cape CanaveralReported only

ULA's Vulcan rocket fails, destroying 40 Amazon Leo satellites

A United Launch Alliance Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after lifting off from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station on Sunday, Nov. 15 (12:40 p.m. ET, per Aviation Week), destroying its payload of 40 Amazon Leo (formerly Project Kuiper) broadband satellites. It is the first outright mission failure since ULA, the Boeing and Lockheed Martin joint venture, was formed in 2006. In a statement, ULA said the Vulcan "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight and failed to deliver the Amazon mission to orbit" and that it would "work closely with our customer and other appropriate organizations to find the cause, determine the appropriate corrective action and return to flight as quickly as possible." The cause has not been announced. It was Vulcan's fifth flight, its first in nine months after work on solid-booster nozzle problems, and the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought for Leo (Spaceflight Now).

Quick facts

  • 40 Amazon Leo satellites lost; failure about 10 minutes into flight
  • ULA's first mission failure in its roughly 20-year history
  • Cause unknown; ULA investigating with Amazon and others

Not yet confirmed

  • The cause of the anomaly
  • Any FAA statement or FAA-overseen mishap investigation (none found at build time)
  • When Vulcan flies again

U.S. · Washington, DCReported only

Federal prosecutor tells Sen. Elizabeth Warren to preserve records

Aaron Reitz, the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Texas, sent Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) a letter on Sunday, Nov. 15, demanding that she preserve documents and communications tied to what he called her intent to use congressional investigations against private people close to President Trump. Warren is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee in January. Reitz wrote that she appears "eager to wield your future gavel like a tomahawk," cited federal civil-rights statutes 18 U.S.C. 241 and 242, and warned that failing to preserve records could bring sanctions. He gave her until 6 p.m. ET on Nov. 23 to confirm in writing that she received the notice and is preserving the material. Sens. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) and Elissa Slotkin (D-Mich.) received similar demands last week. The letter is not a charge, and no charges have been announced. Critics, including legal commentators cited by Politico and others, point to the Constitution's Speech or Debate Clause, which protects legitimate congressional investigations.

Quick facts

  • Sender: Aaron Reitz, U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Texas; letter dated Sunday, Nov. 15 (NBC, Politico)
  • Deadline: 6 p.m. ET, Nov. 23, to confirm in writing that the records are preserved (Fox News, NBC)
  • Cites 18 U.S.C. 241 and 242; warns of sanctions or "significant legal consequences" (Fox News)
  • Similar demands went to Sens. Blumenthal and Slotkin last week (NBC; Democracy Docket)

What happens next

  • Warren's written reply is due by 6 p.m. ET on Nov. 23 (Fox News, NBC)
  • No charges have been announced (Fox News)

U.S. · New OrleansReported only

NTSB: the tanker's rudder stuck about 2 minutes before it hit the New Orleans cruise ship

The National Transportation Safety Board said Sunday that a preliminary review of the voyage data recorder from the tanker Atlantic Asphalt shows a "steering casualty" at about 12:26 p.m. on Friday, Nov. 13, that left its rudder stuck to starboard (to the right). The tanker veered toward the riverbank and struck the docked American Cruise Lines riverboat American Heritage about two minutes later, at about 12:28 p.m. Investigators have not yet determined what caused the steering failure. The NTSB said the riverboat capsized in about 22 minutes and that no one died.

Quick facts

  • Steering casualty at about 12:26 p.m. on Friday, Nov. 13; rudder stuck to starboard (NTSB via AP, USA Today)
  • Tanker Atlantic Asphalt hit the docked riverboat American Heritage about two minutes later (AP)
  • NTSB downloaded more than 9 hours of voyage data recorder data (NTSB briefing; UPI)
  • No deaths; the riverboat capsized in about 22 minutes (NTSB briefing)

Not yet confirmed

  • What caused the steering failure (AP, USA Today)
