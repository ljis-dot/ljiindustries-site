Four-point peace declaration after first Trump-Xi-Putin meeting, no Ukraine ceasefire

President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin held their first three-way meeting Thursday in Shenzhen, China, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. BBC, the Guardian and the Associated Press reported that the talks lasted about 80 to 90 minutes and that Trump then left China. Chinese and Russian state media published a four-point "Declaration toward Peace." BBC treated the document as a joint U.S.-China-Russia statement. The Guardian noted that the text appeared first from Chinese state media and that Russia then issued the same document, and the Associated Press was more cautious, saying China and Russia say the United States agreed. The text, as carried by those outlets, calls for U.N. reform, an increase in the production and supply of refined petroleum products and stable energy flows, a statement that the International Criminal Court and similar bodies must not "abuse" their jurisdiction, and a pledge that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought," plus dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. No Ukraine ceasefire was announced. BBC reported that Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Ukraine was not the main topic. Earlier on Thursday, Trump and Putin met for about 40 minutes; Trump called Putin "my friend," and a Kremlin aide said the talks went well but produced no major statements. The meeting was held at Kylin Villa.

Quick facts

  • First Trump, Xi and Putin meeting, held Thursday in Shenzhen; about 80 to 90 minutes
  • Trump has left China
  • Chinese and Russian state media published a four-point declaration on peace
  • The A.P. says China and Russia say the United States agreed
  • No Ukraine ceasefire was announced

Not yet confirmed

  • A formal, standalone White House release of the same declaration text
  • Whether any unannounced commitments were made in the closed session
  • Whether the energy, U.N. or nuclear-talks language leads to concrete follow-up

What happens next

  • Watch for a U.S. readout, or a matching White House text of the declaration
  • Watch for any follow-up on Ukraine, energy supplies or nuclear-risk talks

U.S. · NationwideReported only

AAA projects 81.5 million Thanksgiving travelers, with average airfare $800, up 17%

AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home for Thanksgiving between Tuesday, Nov. 24, and Monday, Nov. 30. That is about 121,000 fewer than last year, a 0.1% decline, ending a three-year streak of record Thanksgiving travel, according to a forecast AAA released on Nov. 16. About 72.9 million people are expected to drive and 5.96 million to fly domestically. Booked round-trip domestic flights are averaging $800, up 17% from a year earlier, AAA said. The Associated Press also reported the forecast. This briefing notes a national average around $3.75 a gallon only as context for road-trip costs, not as a new AAA pump-price report.

Quick facts

  • 81.5 million travelers projected Nov. 24–30, slightly below last year (AAA)
  • Booked domestic round-trip fares average $800, up 17%
  • About 72.9 million are expected to drive; 5.96 million to fly
  • The forecast was released Nov. 16

Not yet confirmed

  • How close actual volumes and fares will come to the forecast
  • How weather, gas prices or airline operations will affect travel next week

What happens next

  • The Thanksgiving travel window opens Tuesday, Nov. 24
  • Travelers should check flights, roads and weather before they leave

Anthropic sets I.P.O. share price at $83 and raises $81.3 billion; ANT to start trading today

Anthropic set the share price of its initial public offering at $83 on Wednesday and sold 980 million shares, raising $81.3 billion, according to the company, CNBC and the Associated Press. That was above the earlier indicated range of $70 to $78, and it implied a fully diluted value of about $1.5 trillion. The company said the shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on Thursday, Nov. 19, under the ticker ANT, with the offering expected to close on Nov. 20. As of early Thursday morning, trading had not yet begun. This briefing covers the offer size and share price only, not the first trade, which is due after the 9:30 a.m. Eastern open.

Quick facts

  • Share price set at $83; 980 million shares sold; $81.3 billion raised (Anthropic)
  • Fully diluted value of about $1.5 trillion
  • Set to begin trading today on Nasdaq as ANT, after the 9:30 a.m. ET open
  • This briefing does not cover the first trade

Not yet confirmed

  • The opening print and how the shares trade after the market opens
  • Whether underwriters exercise their option to buy additional shares

What happens next

  • Trading is scheduled to start after 9:30 a.m. Eastern
  • The offering is expected to close on Nov. 20, Anthropic said

U.S. · WashingtonReported only

House Democrats nominate Hakeem Jeffries for speaker, putting him on track to be the first Black speaker

House Democrats nominated Rep. Hakeem Jeffries for speaker on Wednesday in a closed-door, secret-ballot vote of 251-11, with no challenger, NBC News, NPR and ABC News reported. He is on track to be the first Black speaker of the House when the full chamber votes on Jan. 3, 2027. NBC projects Democrats will hold 259 seats, enough that Jeffries could absorb a large number of defections and still reach the 218 votes needed. Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts was chosen as majority leader, and Rep. Pete Aguilar of California as whip. Seven of the 11 Democrats who voted no later issued a joint statement saying talks with Jeffries were continuing.

Quick facts

  • Caucus vote Wednesday: 251-11, no challenger
  • Full House vote is Jan. 3, 2027; Jeffries would be the first Black speaker
  • NBC projects Democrats will hold 259 seats
  • Katherine Clark was chosen as majority leader; Pete Aguilar as whip

Not yet confirmed

  • The exact first-ballot tally on Jan. 3
  • Whether any additional Democrats will withhold support on the House floor

What happens next

  • The new Congress convenes and elects a speaker on Jan. 3, 2027
  • Jeffries has said Democrats will focus on affordability and the cost of living

U.S. · WashingtonReported only

Fed Governor Waller says a half-point December rate increase may be needed to "shock people"

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said in a CNBC interview on Wednesday that a half-percentage-point increase in the Fed's benchmark rate is probably necessary at the Dec. 8-9 meeting, unless the Fed raises rates by a quarter point and clearly signals another increase in January. "You might need to, like, shock people," Waller said, adding that inflation is "headed in the wrong direction, and it's spreading out." Decrypt also reported the remarks. Waller said Chairman Kevin Warsh has been against that kind of forward guidance. CNBC said futures still pointed to a quarter-point increase in December, with only a small chance of a half-point move. The consumer effect would be higher borrowing costs heading into the holidays and the new year.

Quick facts

  • Waller: a 50-basis-point increase may be needed at the Dec. 8-9 meeting
  • Alternative: a quarter-point increase plus a clear signal of another hike in January
  • Waller said inflation is headed in the wrong direction and spreading out
  • Markets were still treating a quarter-point move as much more likely (CNBC)

Not yet confirmed

  • What the Federal Open Market Committee will actually decide in December
  • Whether Chair Warsh or a majority would support a half-point increase

What happens next

  • The FOMC meets Dec. 8-9
  • Other Fed officials will keep speaking before that meeting

U.S. · MinneapolisReported only

Target sales rose 7.1%; the company is offering a $30 Thanksgiving meal for six

Target reported third-quarter net sales of $27.1 billion, up 7.1% from a year earlier, with comparable sales up 5.6% and comparable traffic up 5.0%, according to the company's earnings release. Fox Business also reported the results and said Target raised its full-year sales outlook to around 6% growth. GAAP and adjusted earnings were $2.65 a share, including $0.50 a share from tariff refund benefits. Target said it is offering a $30 Thanksgiving meal for six, along with more than 8,000 new gift items, thousands of them priced at $5 or under. November net sales were in line with the company's guidance and accelerating from October, Target said.

Quick facts

  • Third-quarter net sales $27.1 billion, up 7.1%; comparable sales up 5.6%
  • Comparable traffic up 5.0%
  • Full-year sales outlook raised to around 6% growth
  • Offering a $30 Thanksgiving meal for six

Not yet confirmed

  • How strong holiday sales will be in the fourth quarter
  • How much of the earnings figure, excluding tariff refunds, will carry into next year

What happens next

  • The Thanksgiving meal offer is part of Target's holiday period
  • Walmart's results, due this morning, will offer another read on holiday demand
